Creating tournaments and leagues is part of PickleCue Pro. Playing in them, and following them, is free.

One event, end to end.

  1. 01 Set up

    Build the competition.

    A tournament or a league, with the format you actually run. You are not choosing between what the software supports and what your event is.

    • TournamentsSingle elimination, double elimination, round robin
    • LeaguesA season, with a table that keeps itself
    • Singles or doublesIncluding partner pairing at registration

  2. 02 Fill

    Get players in without collecting them by hand.

    Share a link or a printed code. People register themselves, sign the waiver in the same flow, and arrive already on your list.

    • Public registrationA browser form — no account needed to sign up
    • WaiverSigned during registration, stored with the entry
    • PartnersRegister solo or bring one
    • Check-inWho has arrived, who still owes a waiver

  3. 03 Run

    Know what is live, what is next, and what needs you.

    One screen for every event you are running: how many matches are active, how many players are in, and an explicit answer to “is anything waiting on me?” — rather than you going to look.

  4. 04 Advance

    One result moves the competition.

    Enter a score once. The winner takes their place in the next round and the league table reorders itself. Nobody redraws a bracket on a whiteboard, and nobody is working from last night's version.

  5. 05 Share

    Hand people a page instead of a screenshot.

    Print the QR or read the six-character code out. Players and spectators open the event in a browser and stop asking you what the score is.

    • No appIt is an ordinary web page
    • No accountNothing to sign up for to watch
    • What they getSchedule, their next match, live scores, the bracket

    See what a spectator sees

Hand off the scoring, not the event.

You do not have to be at every court. Give someone a court and they can keep score from their own phone — without becoming an organizer, and without being able to change anything else.

They score, you run it

A scorekeeper enters points for the court they were handed. Everything else stays yours.

The number moves everywhere

Players’ phones, the public page, the board at the venue and the Lock Screen, at the same moment.

You can still correct it

A finished match can be fixed from the organizer side when somebody taps the wrong number.
